## 1. Sources

### SRE, delivery performance, ops philosophy

1. **Google SRE Book, ch. 4 "Service Level Objectives"**: https://sre.google/sre-book/service-level-objectives/ (book, fetched).
   Have as few SLOs as possible and start from what users care about. Use percentiles, not averages. Never aim for
   100 %; the error budget drives release decisions. Keep internal targets tighter than the published SLA.
2. **Google SRE Workbook, table of contents**: https://sre.google/workbook/table-of-contents/ (book, snippet). Its
   appendices include an example SLO document and an example error-budget policy, ready-made templates a solo founder
   can copy instead of inventing process.
3. **DORA 2025 year in review**: https://dora.dev/insights/dora-2025-year-in-review/ (research, fetched). AI
   "improves throughput, but often at the cost of stability if your foundation isn't solid". DORA moved from four
   delivery metrics to five and published an AI Capabilities Model with seven capabilities.
4. **Google Cloud, "Announcing the 2025 DORA report"**:
   https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/ai-machine-learning/announcing-the-2025-dora-report (research announcement,
   snippet). About 5,000 respondents; 90 % use AI at work. AI is an amplifier: it does not fix a weak team or system,
   it magnifies what is there.
5. **Dan McKinley, "Choose Boring Technology"**: https://mcfunley.com/choose-boring-technology (essay, fetched).
   "Let's say every company gets about three innovation tokens." Every additional technology adds operating cost
   (monitoring, expertise, failure modes), so optimize for the whole system, not per problem.

### Incident postmortems (B2B SaaS)

6. **Atlassian, Post-Incident Review, April 2022 outage**:
   https://www.atlassian.com/engineering/post-incident-review-april-2022-outage (primary postmortem, fetched). A script
   given site IDs instead of app IDs deleted 883 sites (775 customers); outages lasted up to 14 days. RPO (1 h) was
   met; RTO was missed. Restores were slow because data stores are **shared across tenants**, so each customer's data
   had to be extracted from backups by hand (about 70 steps). Fixes: universal soft delete, no hard deletes from
   scripts, automated multi-tenant restore, DR drills, and customer contact data kept outside the product.
7. **GitLab, "Postmortem of database outage of January 31" (2017)**:
   https://about.gitlab.com/blog/postmortem-of-database-outage-of-january-31/ (primary postmortem, fetched). An
   engineer wiped the primary's data directory. Every backup layer was broken: pg_dump to S3 failed silently (binary
   version mismatch, alert emails rejected by DMARC), and DB disk snapshots were not enabled. About 18 h of downtime
   and about 6 h of data lost. Root causes they named: **no owner of data durability and no routine restore testing**.
8. **The Register, "Salesforce root cause" (May 2021)**: https://www.theregister.com/2021/05/19/salesforce_root_cause
   (press, citing Salesforce's RCA, fetched). A DNS config change went out globally through the emergency break-fix
   path, which bypassed the staggered rollout. A 4-year-old script had a bug, and the recovery tooling depended on DNS
   (circular dependency). Logins were unreliable for about 5 h. Fixes: block manual global deploys, remove the
   recovery tools' dependency on DNS.
9. **ThousandEyes, Salesforce DNS disruption, 16 May 2024**:
   https://www.thousandeyes.com/blog/salesforce-dns-disruption-analysis-may-16-2024 (third-party analysis, snippet).
   Salesforce attributed it to a third-party DNS provider, a reminder that dependencies, not only your own code,
   cause outages.

### Scale, cells, sharding

10. **Slack, "Slack's migration to a cellular architecture"**:
    https://slack.engineering/slacks-migration-to-a-cellular-architecture/ (eng blog, fetched). Triggered by a gray
    failure in one AZ, with a 99.99 % SLA; the migration took **about 1.5 years** across many services. The goal is to
    drain an AZ in under 5 minutes. A large-company response to a large-company problem.
11. **AWS, Guidance for Cell-Based Architecture on AWS**:
    https://docs.aws.amazon.com/solutions/cell-based-architecture-on-aws/ (vendor docs, snippet). Cells are
    independent replicas behind a routing layer, used for fault isolation and blast-radius control.
12. **Notion, "Herding elephants: lessons learned from sharding Postgres at Notion" (2021)**:
    https://www.notion.com/blog/sharding-postgres-at-notion (eng blog, fetched). Notion ran on **one Postgres for about
    5 years while usage grew about 4 orders of magnitude**. Triggers were VACUUM stalls, CPU spikes and the risk of
    transaction-ID wraparound. 480 logical shards (schemas) on 32 machines; **shard key = workspace id**. Their advice
    is "Shard earlier" but not too early.
13. **Figma, "How Figma's databases team lived to tell the scale" (2024)**:
    https://www.figma.com/blog/how-figmas-databases-team-lived-to-tell-the-scale/ (eng blog, snippet). Vertical
    partitioning came first, horizontal sharding only when tables reached several TB and billions of rows. They kept
    Postgres and rejected CockroachDB, TiDB, Spanner and Vitess. Cost: no cross-shard foreign keys or unique indexes.

### Queues and background jobs on Postgres

14. **Brandur Leach, "River: a fast, robust job queue for Go + Postgres"**: https://brandur.org/river (eng blog,
    fetched). The killer feature is **transactional enqueue**: the job exists only if the data commits. One dependency
    stack. Informal figure: about 10k trivial jobs/s on a laptop. Sharp edges: table bloat from long-running
    transactions.
15. **Graphile Worker, Performance**: https://worker.graphile.org/docs/performance (OSS docs, fetched) **[vendor
    benchmark]**. About 15.6k jobs/s without batching and about 184k jobs/s with batching, for trivial jobs with the DB
    on the same machine. The project says it is not meant to replace high-scale dedicated queues.
16. **DBOS, "Just use Postgres for queues / making Postgres queues scale"**:
    https://www.dbos.dev/blog/making-postgres-queues-scale (vendor eng blog, fetched) **[vendor]**. Contention appears
    at about 100/s (no row locking), about 1,000/s (REPEATABLE READ serialization failures) and about 8,000/s (index and
    autovacuum CPU). With SKIP LOCKED, READ COMMITTED and partial indexes they report over 30k workflows/s.
17. **Solid Queue (Rails 8 default) README**: https://github.com/rails/solid_queue (OSS docs, fetched).
    Database-backed queue using FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED; running in a separate database is recommended. Now the
    **default** queue in Rails 8, a mainstream framework choosing "DB as queue".
18. **On Rails podcast, Rosa Gutiérrez on Solid Queue (June 2025)**:
    https://rubyonrails.org/2025/6/24/on-rails-rosa-gutierrez-solid-queue (practitioner interview, snippet).
    37signals moved live apps, including HEY, from Resque/Redis to Solid Queue to cut moving parts.

## 2. Scale a DR/LATAM CRM-with-ERP must handle in years 1-3, and which choices matter

### 2.1 Sizing **[estimate, derived from the sources noted]**

- **Market ceiling in DR.** 100,000 e-CF emitters exist (38) and the last cohort's deadline is 15 Nov 2026 (38).
  Incumbents with distribution are already there: Alegra has DR e-CF content (37), and Odoo partners operate there.
  Siigo, the LATAM leader, took about 20 years and acquisitions to reach about 1.3M customers across 6 countries (36).
- **Realistic solo-founder trajectory.** Year 1: 20-300 paying companies. Year 3: 500-5,000 companies across 1-3
  countries. This is my estimate; no source gives a solo-founder benchmark.
- **Transaction volume at year 3, upper end.** 5,000 companies × about 500 fiscal documents/month = 2.5M/month, about
  1 doc/s on average. A 10-20× month-end peak gives about 10-20 docs/s, which matches the spec's 20 docs/s planning
  peak (§12).
- **Background jobs.** Even at 20-50 jobs per document (submit, poll DGII status, deliver to buyer, email, webhook,
  ledger posting), peak load is about 1,000 jobs/s or less. That sits below the contention points DBOS reports at
  roughly 1,000-8,000/s before tuning (16), and far below what River, Graphile and DBOS report after tuning
  (14, 15, 16).
- **Data volume.** The CRM side (contacts, activities, emails, notes) will outgrow the fiscal side in row count,
  especially if email or WhatsApp activity capture is added. Even at about 300 MB per tenant × 5,000 tenants, that is
  about 1.5 TB. Notion's planning limits were 500 GB per table and 10 TB per physical database (12), so this is one
  well-run Postgres plus object storage for fiscal XML/PDF. Notion grew about 10,000× on one Postgres before sharding
  (12).

**Conclusion: years 1-3 are a single-region, single-primary Postgres workload.** Throughput is not the risk. The risks
are **data loss, bad deletes, bad deploys, missed fiscal deadlines and per-tenant recovery** (6, 7, 8).

### 2.3 What is premature (defer, and do not let agents build it)

- **Cells, multi-region, active-active.** Slack needed 1.5 years and runs a 99.99 % SLA (10). AWS frames cells as
  blast-radius tooling for large fleets (11). A DR SMB product at 99.9 % does not need them. One region plus
  cross-region backup copies is the right DR posture. The spec already defers region choice (Q3).
- **Sharding, distributed SQL, Kafka/SQS.** Notion and Figma reached TB-scale tables first (12, 13). Postgres queues
  carry thousands of jobs/s (14-16).
- **Read replicas and a cache tier (Redis).** Multi-AZ standbys do not serve reads (22). Add one read replica only when
  reporting/BI queries start to hurt OLTP. Caching is the last resort for a product this size.
- **Managed Kubernetes plus Helm plus network policies for five processes.** This is a judgment call; I found no source
  comparing K8s and PaaS for solo founders. By McKinley's token logic (5), Kubernetes is an innovation token spent on
  infrastructure, not on the product. A managed container platform (ECS/Fargate, Cloud Run, Fly, Render) with the same
  single image gives the same isolation for the signer and receiver at lower cognitive load. The spec's "minimal"
  production is already K8s-shaped (§13); this is the one place where the ops plan is heavier than the scale requires.
- **Self-hosting the identity provider.** If Zitadel is self-hosted, it is a second critical stateful system to back up
  and patch. Use its managed cloud or an equivalent until there is revenue. **[judgment]**
- **Mutation testing and 100 % branch coverage everywhere.** This is not an ops cost, but DORA says AI raises
  throughput and lowers stability unless the foundation is solid (3, 4). The strict gate is that foundation, so keep it
  on fiscal and money paths. It is costly, though, when applied to CRM UI breadth. Other researchers cover this.

## 3. Operational burden a solo founder can carry

**Carry (about 1-3 h/week steady state) [estimate]:**
- One cloud region. One managed Postgres (Multi-AZ, PITR, automated minor upgrades). One object-storage bucket with
  object lock. One container platform running **one image** with 2-3 process types (api plus receiver, worker; the
  signer only where key custody demands it). One managed IdP. One observability vendor that accepts OpenTelemetry
  (traces and metrics are stable in Python (23); logs are still maturing).
- **Three to five paging alerts, all symptom-based, following SRE ch. 4 (1):**
  - API availability burn rate;
  - e-CF not final past its threshold while DGII is up (the fiscal SLO);
  - DGII contingency window above 50 % consumed;
  - certificate or numbering range near expiry;
  - backup/WAL archive lag.

  Everything else in the spec's §11 alert list (dead inbox rows, unrouted outbox, parked jobs) should open a ticket or
  a daily digest, not a page. A solo founder cannot answer 15 pagers at night.
- **A monthly automated restore drill** into a scratch instance that verifies row counts per tenant (7, 21). **A
  per-tenant export/restore tool** (6).
- **Copy the SLO doc and error-budget policy from the SRE Workbook (2).** When the budget is burned, stop feature work
  and fix reliability. That is the only "process" a team of one needs.
- **Status page and customer-contact list hosted outside the product** (6: Atlassian lost the ability to contact
  customers because contact data was inside the deleted sites).

**Cannot carry, so buy or avoid:**
- 24/7 on-call. The 99.9 % SLO allows about 43 min/month of downtime. DGII's contingency regime and an offline-capable
  issuing path matter more than chasing nines.
- Running Kafka, Kubernetes operators, service mesh, self-hosted Postgres HA, self-hosted IdP, or multi-region failover.
- Hand-run production changes. Everything goes through CI, staged, with a rollback (8).

**Cost [estimate; no primary per-tenant benchmark found]:**
- Infrastructure for year 1 is a fixed floor (Multi-AZ Postgres, containers, object storage, observability, IdP)
  measured in hundreds, not thousands, of USD per month. At 500-5,000 tenants the marginal infrastructure cost per SMB
  tenant is cents to low single dollars per month.
- Secondary benchmarks put hosting at about 8 % of spend under $1M ARR (24) and private-SaaS gross margin at about
  73 % (25).
- The variable cost that can actually hurt margins is **LLM inference for AI features**. Incumbents meter it
  separately: credits (26, 29) or per-outcome fees (27). Plan usage metering into the billing model from day one.

## 4. Where the market is going, and what architecture that demands

**Observed trends (sourced):**
1. **Agents inside the CRM, priced per use or outcome, not per seat.**
   - Salesforce: $2/conversation, $0.10/action, Flex Credits (26).
   - HubSpot: moved from credits to $0.50 per *resolved* conversation and $1 per lead in April 2026 (27).
   - Attio: seats plus pooled workspace credits plus credit packs (29).
   - Odoo keeps cheap per-user pricing (€19.90-35) with AI bundled in v20 (35).

   Pricing is now hybrid: seat plus metered AI.
2. **The CRM is exposed to outside agents through MCP.** HubSpot ships a hosted, OAuth-scoped MCP server that powers
   its Claude, ChatGPT, Copilot and Gemini connectors (28). Buyers will expect "works from my AI assistant".
3. **Flexible data models.** AI-native CRMs (Attio (30), Twenty (31)) are metadata-driven: users define objects and
   fields, and APIs are generated from metadata.
4. **Unified data layer.**
   - Salesforce sells "zero-copy" Data 360 as the agent's substrate (32).
   - Clay's $3.1bn → $7.1bn valuation (33) shows the money flowing to enrichment and data platforms.
5. **Skepticism is warranted.** Gartner expects 40 %+ of agentic projects to be canceled by 2027 and calls out "agent
   washing" (34). Agents must deliver measurable outcomes, which is exactly what HubSpot's outcome pricing encodes (27).
6. **LATAM consolidation.**
   - Siigo is building one SME ecosystem (accounting, payroll, sales/POS) through acquisitions plus $100M (36).
   - Alegra is in 19+ countries and raising for AI (37).
   - Odoo is growing about 48 %/yr at 28M users (35).

   The competitors a DR newcomer faces are funded, multi-country, and already AI-marketing.

**What that demands of the architecture:**
- **A metadata/extensibility layer.** Custom objects and fields with generated APIs and UI (31), so agents and users
  can discover and extend the schema. This is the single biggest gap relative to the market, and the earlier jury
  flagged the same gap ("no metadata layer").
- **An agent-grade API surface.**
  - Fine-grained OAuth scopes acting "as the user" (28).
  - Idempotency keys on every write.
  - Dry-run/preview for destructive actions, soft delete (6), and a complete audit trail of who or what (human or
    agent) did what.
  - An MCP server wrapping that API.

  The repo already has idempotency, audit events, RFC 9457 errors and test/live modes. These are strong foundations
  for agent safety.
- **Usage metering and an entitlement service** (credits, per-outcome billing) (26, 27, 29).
- **An activity/event timeline per customer**, fed by the outbox, as the context store for agents and a later
  analytics replica or warehouse export. No separate data platform is needed in years 1-3: a nightly or CDC export to
  a warehouse-format bucket covers the "data platform" story at this scale. **[judgment]**
- **The LLM boundary as a port.** The provider is swappable and calls are metered per tenant. Tenant data never leaves
  in logs (as the repo already mandates for tax ids and payloads).

## 5. Verdict (5 bullets)

- **Scale is not the problem.** Years 1-3 for a DR-first CRM-with-ERP is about 1-20 fiscal docs/s, under about
  1,000 jobs/s, and at most a few TB: one region, one Postgres primary. The repo's core runtime choices are correct
  for that scale and are not the wrong path: Postgres outbox/inbox and jobs, RLS by tenant, idempotency, PITR,
  object-lock archive, OTel plus SLOs (12, 14-17, 21). Cells, sharding, Kafka, Redis, read replicas and multi-region
  are premature (10-13, 22).
- **Spend reliability effort where B2B SaaS actually fails:** bad deletes, untested backups and unstaged global changes
  (6, 7, 8). Add a per-tenant export and restore, universal soft delete, a monthly automated restore drill with
  WAL-archive monitoring, and staged rollouts. These are cheap now and impossible to retrofit during an incident.
- **The ops plan is heavier than a solo founder can carry.** Replace "managed Kubernetes plus Helm plus five
  processes plus a long pager list" with a managed container platform, one image and 2-3 process types, a managed IdP,
  and 3-5 symptom-based pages driven by an error-budget policy (1, 2, 5). Keep the signer isolated only as far as key
  custody legally requires.
- **The market gap is product architecture, not infrastructure.**
  - Buyers and competitors now expect custom objects (metadata-driven, as in Attio and Twenty) (30, 31), an
    agent-safe API exposed over MCP (28), usage-metered AI (26, 27, 29) and an AI-ready customer timeline.
  - The chassis lacks the metadata layer and the UI. That is where the next months should go, or where you should buy
    (Odoo/ERPNext-class platforms already provide it (35)).
- **Compete narrowly and measurably.**
  - The DR window is closing: 100k emitters, last cohort due 15 Nov 2026 (38).
  - Funded LATAM consolidators (Siigo, Alegra, Odoo) are moving in with AI (35, 36, 37), and Gartner warns that
    agent hype gets canceled (34).
  - Lead with the reliable fiscal engine plus a few outcome-priced agents (e.g. "invoice accepted by DGII", "collections
    follow-up"), not with ERP breadth or infrastructure ambition.
