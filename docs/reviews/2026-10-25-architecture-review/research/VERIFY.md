## 5-line summary for P1 vs P2

1. Frappe is MIT but its Postgres support is framework-level only, with no stable-support statement, and ERPNext on Postgres only just got an opt-in nightly CI (June 2026) with failing queries reported in March 2026: P2 on the owner's Postgres is a bet on an unofficial path, so it would mean running MariaDB beside the Postgres engine (two database technologies).
2. Neither ERPNext nor official Odoo has an e-CF localization (ERPNext has no DR folder at all; Odoo's `l10n_do` only has NCF/accounting), so the DR e-CF engine you already built is the scarce piece in both options, and third-party Odoo e-CF modules sell for about USD 60-300.
3. Licences: Frappe (MIT) is permissive, ERPNext is GPL-3, Odoo CE is LGPL-3, Twenty is AGPL plus commercial files; a Frappe app that depends on ERPNext likely inherits GPL-3 (needs legal advice), while a pure Frappe app does not.
4. The market facts favour a deadline-driven engine: 100k emitters (Oct 2026), ~190-200 providers, deadline 15 Nov 2026 for the last cohort, but about 64% of emitters use DGII's free facturador, so a paid product must sell breadth/integration (ERP, CRM, accounting), not bare e-CF emission; no public per-document price was found to anchor pricing.
5. So P1 keeps full control of Postgres and the existing engine but must build metadata, UI and reports from nothing; P2 buys breadth fast but on MariaDB, with unproven Frappe-on-Postgres and no DR localization; a middle path the evidence supports is to keep the engine as a service and test breadth on Frappe/Odoo, which matches the earlier jury's 5-day experiment, but this is a judgement, not a sourced fact.
