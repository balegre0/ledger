## 2. Recommended data architecture for a CRM with ERP features

The audience is a solo founder plus AI agents, selling to Dominican SMEs first and other countries later. Volumes per
tenant are small: a DR SME issues hundreds to tens of thousands of documents a month. The binding constraints are
**customizability, reporting and correctness**, not raw throughput. **[R3 inference from CONTEXT.md market figures]**

### 2.1 Tenancy: pool first, with stamps and cells as the escape hatch

- **Pool model**: shared tables, `organization_id` (plus company and mode) on every row, and RLS as defence in depth.
  It is the cheapest, onboards with no new infrastructure, and the shared-table design scales to thousands to tens of
  thousands of tenants [2][12].
- **Not schema-per-tenant, not database-per-tenant, as the default.** Database per tenant suits "5 or 50 tenants" [12].
  Schema per tenant has pool's noisy-neighbour problems plus per-tenant provisioning and migration fan-out [2].
  Twenty does it, but runs a migration engine just for that [22]. Odoo and ERPNext are database-per-tenant, which is
  why their DDL-based custom fields work [24][25].
- **Design for a later split**: no global unique keys, the tenant id in every PK, FK and unique constraint (FK and
  unique checks bypass RLS [7]), and IDs that do not depend on one sequence. Instagram [17], Notion [13] and Figma [16]
  each paid to retrofit exactly these.
- **Escape hatches, in order** (thresholds from [8][13][14][16]):
  1. **Vertical split by module** (move a module's schema to its own instance) when one module dominates IO. Figma did
     about a dozen of these before sharding [16].
  2. **Horizontal split by organization**, through Citus row-based distribution [10] or app-level logical shards
     (Notion: 480 schemas on 32 to 96 hosts [13][14]). Triggers:
     - any table approaching ~0.5–1 TB, or the database ~5–10 TB (Notion's planning limits: 500 GB per table, 10 TB
       per DB [13]);
     - sustained peak CPU above ~70–90% [14];
     - provisioned IOPS near their limit [14][16];
     - autovacuum unable to keep up, or XID age climbing towards the 200M forced-freeze default [8][13];
     - connection limits at the pooler [14].
  3. **Stamps or cells per region** when a customer or law demands data residency, or when blast radius matters for
     enterprise contracts. Slack ended up with six residency regions [18], Shopify with 100+ pods [20], Azure
     recommends stamps from day one [4], and AWS describes cells [6]. Route by organization (or by tax id, as the
     spec's cell router intends).
- **Silo (dedicated cluster) is a price tier, not an architecture.** Bridge [1] lets a large customer get its own
  stamp running the same code.

### 2.2 Custom fields and custom objects (the CRM-defining capability)

This is the part where a CRM differs from an invoicing engine. Options, from the evidence:

| Approach | Who | Fits when | Breaks when |
|---|---|---|---|
| Real columns through runtime DDL | Odoo [25], ERPNext [24], Twenty (per-workspace schema) [22] | Database or schema per tenant | Shared tables: per-tenant columns are an explicit antipattern [4] |
| Preset flex columns `Value0..N` plus pivot index tables | Salesforce [21] | Massive scale with a big platform team | Too much machinery for a solo founder: it means building your own index and uniqueness subsystem |
| EAV rows (entity, attribute, value) | Classic | Rarely filtered attributes | Reporting and filtering need N self-joins, and the planner fails **[well known; no single source fetched]** |
| **JSONB column plus a metadata registry** | Citus recommends it over flex columns [12]; Heap warns about its limits [23] | Sparse, per-tenant attributes; thousands of tenants | Hot fields filtered or aggregated in large tables, because there are no planner statistics [23] |

**Recommendation (hybrid):**

- **Metadata registry tables**: object definitions, field definitions (type, validation, options, required, default,
  label translations), views/layouts, and permissions per field. All tenant-scoped, versioned and cached. This is what
  Twenty [22] and Salesforce [21] both have, and what the current repo lacks.
- **Custom fields on standard objects**: one `custom jsonb` column per extensible table, validated against the
  registry on write. Use expression or GIN indexes only for fields a tenant marks "filterable". Promote a field that is
  filtered across many tenants to a real column in a product release (expand/contract), never per tenant.
- **Custom objects**: a generic `records(organization_id, object_id, id, data jsonb, …)` table plus a
  `record_links(organization_id, from_id, to_id, link_def_id)` table. This is Salesforce's `MT_Data` plus
  `MT_Relationships` in Postgres form [21].
- **Escalation threshold**: add pivot or index tables or move reporting to OLAP when:
  - a custom-object table passes roughly 10⁷–10⁸ rows; or
  - per-tenant filters on custom fields show p95 latency regressions.

  **[R3 inference; no source gives a universal number]**
- **Everything downstream reads the registry**: the API schema (OpenAPI or GraphQL generated per tenant, as in
  Twenty [22]), import/export, list views, filters and reports. This is the "metadata layer" the earlier jury said is
  missing.

### 2.3 Ledger (accounting core)

- **Double-entry in the same Postgres**, inside the monolith, for ACID with invoicing [26][27][28]:
  - `journal_entries` plus `journal_lines`, at least two lines, balanced per currency (checked by a deferred
    constraint trigger);
  - insert-only, with corrections by reversal or delta entry [28];
  - pending to posted lifecycle (pending mutable, posted immutable) [28][29];
  - balances derived from lines, cached per account and period with an optimistic version [27];
  - an idempotency key per source event.

  Spec §7.9 already says almost exactly this.
- **Stripe-style clearing checks** [26] as continuous data-quality invariants. Examples: an "invoices issued but not
  posted" clearing account must reach zero, AR subledger equals GL control account, and the e-CF accepted total equals
  revenue posted per period.
- **TigerBeetle is not needed** [29]. It targets very high-throughput transfer processing. SME ERP volume is orders of
  magnitude below that. Reconsider only for wallet or payments products. **[R3 inference]**
- **Business documents (orders, invoices) are mutable objects; ledger lines are not** [28].

### 2.4 Audit and immutability

Three layers, each with a different purpose:

1. **Legal archive**: WORM object storage, 10 years. The repo already has this, and it is excellent.
2. **Security audit log**: who did what, append-only, hashes. The repo has this.
3. **Field-level change history with values** for users ("who changed this deal's amount from X to Y"). This is a CRM
   table-stakes feature, the Salesforce field-history equivalent. **The repo's `audit_events` deliberately stores
   only hashes**, so the user-facing history must be a separate tenant-scoped table that holds values under RLS, with
   PII retention rules.

### 2.5 Reporting and the cross-module read path (CQRS)

- **Stage 0 (now to ~hundreds of tenants)**:
  - one Postgres, plus a **read replica** for reports;
  - a `reporting` schema of **read-only, versioned SQL views or materialized views** that each module publishes as a
    contract, readable only by a `reporting_ro` role.

  This is the cheapest cross-module read path. It is a single-store CQRS read model [5], and needs no event-fed
  projections for every report.
- **Stage 1 (heavy analytics, dashboards over millions of lines, cross-tenant BI for the vendor)**: add DuckDB in
  process or pg_duckdb [34] on the replica, or Parquet exports.
- **Stage 2 (real-time analytics at scale, embedded customer dashboards)**: CDC from Postgres to ClickHouse [33] or to
  a lake [15]. Notion's lesson: CDC beats connector-per-shard ETL and fits update-heavy workloads [15].
  - Triggers: report queries above ~5–10 s on the replica, or the replica lagging because of analytic load, or tables
    above ~10⁸ rows aggregated interactively. **[R3 inference]**
- **Event-fed projections** (outbox to inbox to read table) are for operational read models that need their own
  shape, such as "customer 360" or pipeline boards. They are not the default for reporting, because every one of them
  is a consumer to build, backfill and rebuild [5].

### 2.6 Search

- Postgres FTS plus `pg_trgm` for CRM global search (names, emails, tax ids, document numbers), RLS-filtered.
- ParadeDB `pg_search` [35] if relevance or faceting is needed, still inside the transaction.
- An external engine (OpenSearch/Typesense) fed by CDC [32] only when search load or features justify the sync
  pipeline. **[R3 inference]**

### 2.7 Migrations

- Expand/contract on every change [31], with the app compatible with N−1 schema versions [4].
- Dual-write, backfill, compare, switch for model changes on large tables [30]. Notion and Figma used the same
  shape [13][16].
- **No per-tenant DDL in shared tables** [4]. Custom fields are data, not schema.
- **Every schema multiplies migration fan-out.** With schema-per-tenant, one release equals N DDL runs; Twenty needs a
  dedicated runner for this [22]. With schema-per-module it is bounded by the module count, which is fine.

## 3. How the current design fits or fails

The current design (spec §6, §7, §9.3; ADR 0004; jury synthesis §5) is:

- one Postgres cluster, a schema and DML role per module;
- RLS on (organization, company, mode), with `NULLIF`, `(SELECT …)` and `FORCE`;
- an outbox, inbox, jobs, idempotency and audit_events set in every schema;
- no cross-schema FK, view or join; cross-module reads only through facades or event-fed read models;
- `metadata` on resources: up to 50 string keys, never indexed;
- Party, accounting, entitlements and the ISV-grant model exist as design only.

### Fits (keep)

| Current choice | Evidence | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| Pool tenancy with `organization_id` on every row as shard key; nothing unique across organizations except the tax-id registry | [2][10][12][13][16][17] | **Exactly right.** This is the invariant Notion, Figma and Instagram had to retrofit. Citus row-based distribution [10] or app-level logical shards stay open. |
| RLS policies with `(SELECT current_setting…)`, leading tenant columns in hot indexes, `FORCE ROW LEVEL SECURITY`, no `BYPASSRLS` role, generated deny-by-default tests | [3][7][9] | **Above industry baseline.** Azure notes many teams skip RLS because it is complex [4]; this repo already paid that cost. |
| Tenant carried in composite FKs (`(organization_id, company_id, mode, x_id)`) | [7] (FK checks bypass RLS) | **Correct and subtle.** It closes the hole the Postgres docs warn about. |
| Schema per **module**, with extraction by logical replication | [16] (vertical partitioning first), [14] (logical replication to move schemas) | **Good.** It is a ready vertical-partitioning seam, Figma's first scaling step. |
| Transactional outbox in Postgres; expand-only migrations during overlap | [5][30][31][32] | **Correct.** Debezium's outbox router [32] is a drop-in upgrade path to a broker. |
| Immutable legal archive; append-only audit; ledger design (§7.9): balanced entries, reversal only, closed periods, idempotent per source | [26][27][28][29] | **Matches Stripe and Modern Treasury practice.** Missing: clearing-account invariants [26] and a pending/posted state [28]. |
| Planned cell router keyed by tax registration | [6][18][20] | **Right direction** for DR-first, multi-country residency later. |

### Fails or gaps (for a CRM with ERP features)

1. **No metadata layer: no custom fields, no custom objects, no per-tenant views or layouts.** The `metadata`
   envelope is Stripe-style: strings only, at most 50 keys, never indexed. It is a good field for API integrators and
   **not** a CRM custom-field system: there are no types, validation, filtering, reporting or UI. This is the single
   largest gap. Every CRM reference (Salesforce [21], Twenty [22], Odoo [25], ERPNext [24]) is built around a
   metadata registry. Retrofitting one later touches the API generation, validation, import, list and filter code
   paths in every module. **[R3 inference]**
2. **No cross-module read path in practice.** The "no view or join across schemas" rule means every report that spans
   modules needs an event-fed projection, with its own consumer, backfill, rebuild and eventual consistency [5].
   Example: sales by customer by product with margin, which spans parties, invoicing, inventory and accounting. An ERP
   is mostly such reports. No projection and no reporting store exist today.
   - Fix: a **published read-only reporting contract** per module (versioned views in a `reporting` schema, read by a
     `reporting_ro` role, on a replica). This is a narrow, deliberate ADR exception, not a free-for-all. Plus CDC to
     OLAP later [15][33][34].
3. **The CRM centre of gravity is not built.** The CRM core is Party, Contact, Account, Deal, Activity. Parties are
   "after go-live", and invoices carry inline snapshots (§7.2). The snapshot is right for fiscal documents. But the
   design has no relationship graph, activity timeline, ownership or territory model, so the "CRM" part of the
   product is entirely future work.
4. **Audit stores hashes, not values.** Right for privacy and the legal log. It cannot back the user-facing field
   history a CRM needs, so a separate value-bearing history is required (§2.4).
5. **Chassis tables per schema multiply.** Each module schema carries outbox, inbox, jobs, subscriptions, idempotency
   and audit tables with aggressive autovacuum settings, and Notion and Figma both hit vacuum as a limit [8][13][16].
   At ~25–30 ERP modules that means ~150+ queue-like hot tables, polled by one relay. That is manageable at SME volume,
   but it is operational surface a solo founder pays for on every module added. **[R3 inference]**
6. **"Mode" (test/live) as a tenancy dimension on every row** doubles the RLS predicate and the index width. It is
   fine and Stripe-proven for an API product. For an ERP UI product, most vendors use separate sandbox tenants instead.
   **[R3 inference]** This is not a blocker.
7. **Search: none designed.** Needs at least Postgres FTS/trigram (§2.6).

**Net:** at the storage and tenancy layer, the current design is a **correct, even exemplary, foundation for a pooled
multi-tenant Postgres SaaS**. It will not need re-architecture to reach thousands of SME tenants; Postgres limits bite
far beyond that for this workload [13][14][16]. What it is **not** is a CRM/ERP *data platform*. It lacks the metadata
layer, the reporting/read path and the CRM entities. Those three are product-defining, not polish, and none of them
conflicts with the chassis. They are additive, but large.

---

## 4. Verdict (5 bullets)

- **Keep the storage foundation.** Postgres pool tenancy, RLS, `organization_id` as the shard key, no global uniques,
  tenant-carrying FKs and outbox are textbook-correct, and better than most SaaS at this stage [2][3][7][10][13][16].
  The "wrong path" fear does not apply at the data-tenancy layer. Postgres will carry thousands of DR SMEs on one
  primary; sharding triggers (~0.5–1 TB tables, CPU above 70–90%, IOPS ceiling, vacuum or XID pressure) are years
  away [8][13][14].
- **The decisive gap is the missing metadata layer.** Build a typed metadata registry, JSONB custom fields with opt-in
  indexes, and generic custom-object records and links (Salesforce's model in Postgres form [21][12][23]). Do not use
  per-tenant DDL in shared tables [4], and do not adopt schema-per-tenant (Twenty's path [22]).
- **Open a sanctioned cross-module read path now.** Each module publishes read-only reporting views on a replica, with
  DuckDB/pg_duckdb next and CDC to ClickHouse or a lake only past ~10⁸-row interactive aggregates [5][15][33][34]. The
  current "events-only read models" rule makes every ERP report an engineering project.
- **Keep the ledger in Postgres and in-process**, as spec §7.9 designs it. Add Stripe-style clearing-account
  invariants and a pending/posted state [26][28]. TigerBeetle or a separate ledger service is unnecessary at SME
  volumes [29].
- **The data architecture is an engine, not yet a CRM/ERP.** Compared with buying (Odoo/ERPNext give a metadata layer,
  reports and UI, but use database-per-tenant with real-column custom fields [24][25]), this repo has stronger
  multi-tenant isolation but none of the CRM/ERP data surfaces. That supports the earlier jury's "engine plus decide
  the ERP layer by experiment" conclusion. If the owner builds the CRM themselves, the metadata registry and reporting
  contract must come **before** the next functional module, not after.
